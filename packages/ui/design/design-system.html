No Origins · Design System · v1

Everything decided, rendered once, explained.

This page is built in the system it documents. The canvas above is the home: an infinite surface — drag it, zoom it — where the blobs live. They breathe, blink and follow your pointer. The clear one is Bhargav. Switch theme and block accent from the nav to see the axes work.

Thesis

Boldness without saturation. The palette is your softest option and the brief says passion, bold, curious. So boldness lives in scale (big type, big blobs), shape (true pills, generous radii), material (glass with real depth) and character (things that look at you and speak). Quiet means uncluttered, never muted.

§2 · Colour

The Sketch palette

Every value is oklch() — perceptually even, so hues at the same lightness genuinely look like siblings. Hex is the fallback.

Roles

The family — seven blobs, one per block

Two tiers, always together

Each hue has a fill (the blob, chips, tints — light) and a deep tier (text, icons, strokes — dark enough to pass 4.5:1 on the ground). Body text is never set in a fill; a fill is never used for text under 18px bold.

Tints are derived, never hand-picked: color-mix(in oklch, var(--hue) 45%, var(--surface)) for bubbles and chips, 22% for large areas.

Block accent

A block sets two variables at its root and nothing else: --accent and --accent-deep. Focus rings, links, the send button and the block's own blob read them. Everything else reads ink.

This page is the portfolio block, so its accent is peach — try the dots in the nav to see the editor, agents or tools blocks.

Semantic

Why

Your references were all light, warm and multi-hued — and your sketch already had seven characters on grey. Your Figma shows how the pastel happens: a saturated hue at 24% over the grey ground — so the system keeps both numbers, the tint for glass and the pastel it reads as for chips and tints, and adds a text-safe deep tier per hue and the rule that one block owns one hue. Dark theme lowers the blobs' lightness so they sit in the room instead of glowing.

§3 · Material

Liquid glass, our way

Glass is a surface, not a style. It exists so the dot grid and the blobs behind a panel stay faintly visible. One recipe, three levels.

glass-1

Bubbles, chips

58% fill · 14px blur · e1. The lightest touch.

glass-2

Nav, input, panels

66% fill · 18px blur · e2. The workhorse — this page's nav.

glass-3

Sheets, dialogs

74% fill · 22px blur · e4. Sits on top of everything.

The recipe

A translucent --surface fill · backdrop-filter: blur() saturate(1.35) · a 1px hairline · a 1px white highlight on the top edge (the edge catches light) · a 1px ink shade on the bottom edge (it sits) · a diagonal specular sweep from the upper-left · a warm shadow.

Dark theme keeps the fills, drops the highlights to 14–22%, and turns the hairline white at 10%.

Rules
  • Glass only over something worth seeing through. On a flat solid ground it's a grey box — use --surface.
  • At most two stacked glass layers. Never three.
  • Never on scrolling lists. One panel, not forty.
  • Budget: ≤ 6 glass elements visible, blur ≤ 22px. Tested on a mid-range phone.
  • Fallbacks are solid, not broken: no backdrop-filter → 96% surface; prefers-reduced-transparency → opaque.
Why

You said you were leaning toward liquid glass, and your Figma avatar is made of it. Glass gives a soft palette its depth and boldness — panels have weight, edges catch light, and the platform stays present behind its UI. The rules exist because glass is expensive and easy to overdo; rationed, it's the material. Everywhere, it's mush.

§4 · The blob

A pill with two eyes

From your sketch and your Figma frame. Three variants: colour character, ink logotype, and the clear-glass host — you.

Geometry — 96 × 64, from Figma
Bodyrect 96 × 64, rx 32 — a true pill (Figma radius 100) Eyesr 10 at (30, 32) and (66, 32) — 20px eyes, gap 16, padding 20 / 22. Tuned in the Blob Lab; wider than the Figma auto-layout. Eye colour#474747 in light; pure black in dark. Hostno fill — white at 5% for presence, rim 0.95, same frost. Glass with nothing in it. Logotypeoutline stroke 5.5, inset half a stroke; eyes in currentColor MaterialSaturated tint at 24% over a 2px frost that desaturates what's behind (saturate 0.6) · light −45°: a 0.8 rim, white 90% top-left easing to 40% on the far side — thin and nearly continuous · no inner shadow — flat glass, not thick · refraction: the grid re-drawn 6% magnified from 32% of the radius outward, at 32% opacity — faint and wide.
Three variants

Every character is tinted glass — a saturated colour at 24%, so the grid is always visible through it. Ink outline = the logotype, inside the wordmark. Glass with no tint = the host — Figma's light −45° · refraction 80 · depth 20 · dispersion 50 · frost 2, approximated in CSS for v1 and done properly with a displacement map or a Skia shader later.

Wordmark

Caps, Bowlby One — its one weight — tracking +0.01em. The two O's are the blob, at 0.82em. In running text the name is lowercase no origins — never both in one line.

Sizes — all 3 : 2

States — live

Why

Every mark I proposed across two rounds was worse than this one, and this one came from you. It's approachable at the maximum, curious by construction (it's looking), and it scales from a favicon to a canvas of characters. It also fits your next block exactly — an agents harness where every agent is a blob. Making the host clear glass means nobody needs a label to find you.

§5 · Typography

Three faces, three jobs

Loaded with next/font, latin subset, swap. Bowlby One carries the personality at large sizes; Hanken does everything else; Mono labels and codes.

Display · Bowlby OneCurious & bold

Poster-heavy, slightly narrow, retro without the softness — the closest free relative of Phosphate Solid, with more bite. One weight: it appears only at 34px and above and in the wordmark, and is never asked for a bold it doesn't have.

Body · Hanken GroteskQuiet & clear

Body, UI, and now h3 and h4 too — a poster face at card-title size would shout. Weights 400 / 500 / 600.

Mono · JetBrains Monolabel · code

Eyebrows, meta, tokens, and the code you'll show. Uppercase labels get +0.10em tracking.

Why

Bricolage was the one thing on this page that wasn't yours, and you said so. Your direction was Phosphate Solid — the blob's shape as letters — and Bowlby One is its closest free relative with more bite. A single-weight poster face sets one rule: it lives at h2 and above and in the wordmark; Hanken takes h3, h4 and everything smaller, so the loud voice is rationed like the glass. Measure stays 60–70 characters; headings balance, paragraphs wrap pretty; digits tabular wherever they line up.

§8 · Layout

Two modes, one ground

The dot grid — 32px gap, 1.4px dots at ink 14% — is on every page in both modes. It's what glass refracts and what the blobs live on.

Canvas — home, the harness · React Flow

An infinite canvas built on @xyflow/react: the dot grid is its <Background variant="dots" gap={32} size={1.4}>, every blob is a custom node, bubbles are node toolbars, the chat input is a <Panel position="bottom-center">. Pan by drag, zoom by wheel and pinch, fitView on load. No edges in v1; no minimap; controls are ours. No nav — the canvas is the nav.

Page — About, Work, Roadmap, Contact

Max-width 1120; gutters 24 / 48; 12 columns, gap 24; reading column ≤ 68ch. Sticky glass-2 nav. Breakpoints 640 / 900 / 1200.

§10 · Theming axes

Four switches, no new CSS

1 · Theme

Light / dark / system, via data-theme and prefers-color-scheme. Tokens swap; nothing else.

2 · Block accent

One hue per block via data-block. Pick one:

3 · Transparency

prefers-reduced-transparency flattens every glass surface to solid --surface. Layout unchanged.

4 · Motion

prefers-reduced-motion removes ambient motion and pointer-following. Blink stays, slower.

Why

Consistency is in your anti-list, so it's enforced structurally. Four independent axes multiply into every combination — a dark, reduced-motion, lavender-accented editor block — without a line of new CSS. That's what “one system, every block” means in practice.

§11–14 · Package, budget, build

Where it lives and how it ships

@no-origins/ui — inside the portfolio repo
portfolio/ ├─ pnpm-workspace.yaml packages: ['packages/*'] ├─ packages/ui/ @no-origins/ui │ └─ src/ │ ├─ tokens.css :root · dark · @theme (Tailwind 4) │ ├─ glass.css motion.css │ ├─ blob/ wordmark/ canvas/ canvas/ = React Flow nodes + panels │ ├─ components/ 15 for v1 │ └─ index.ts └─ src/app/ the portfolio block ├─ layout.tsx next/font ×3 · data-block="portfolio" └─ globals.css @import "@no-origins/ui/tokens.css"

Extracts cleanly when there's a second block. Tailwind utilities like bg-ground, text-peach-deep, rounded-pill, shadow-e2 come from the same token file.

Accessibility & performance budget
  • Ink on ground ≈ 8:1. Every deep tier ≥ 4.5:1 on ground. Fills are never text backgrounds under 18px bold.
  • Focus visible everywhere: 2px accent-deep, 2px offset. Hit targets ≥ 44px.
  • Interactive blobs are buttons with labels; decorative ones are aria-hidden. Bubbles are real text.
  • Hero is type + inline SVG — no images. Only the nav is glass above the fold in page mode.
  • ≤ 6 glass elements visible; blur ≤ 22px. Blob animation is transform/opacity only.
  • Three font families, latin subset, swap, fallbacks with close metrics.

Build order

Blob + Wordmark — every size, every state, every variant, with a fixture page.

NavBar, SectionHeader, Footer — page layout.
The four blocks — About (photo + glass host), Work (four BlockCards), Roadmap (two sleeping blobs), Contact.
